# Design principles for believable game characters

Read this file when you write the character sheet, review a concept, or plan the evolution stages. Each section gives the rule, the reason, and a check.

## 1. The person comes before the picture

- **Know the character before you draw them.** At Supergiant, the art director Jen Zee wants to understand a character deeply before she designs the look. Her designs then sharpen the writer's sense of the character's voice. [Kotaku: the art of Hades](https://kotaku.com/the-art-of-hades-1845443438), [Supergiant on adding Demeter](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon)
- **Each visual element says something about the character.** This was Arnold Tsang's rule for the heroes of Overwatch. If a strap, a scar or a colour says nothing, remove it or give it a reason. [Kotaku on Overwatch character design](https://kotaku.com/1792927498)
- **Start from old, specific sources.** Supergiant goes back to the oldest versions of the myths. For a mercenary, start from a real trade, a custom or a folktale, not from other games. [Hades II artwork](https://creativebloq.com/art/digital-art/hades-ii-artwork)

## 2. The inner core: want, need, wound, flaw, contradiction, secret

The writer David Corbett says a character that feels real has a strong desire, hides a secret, suffers a wound and shows a contradiction. [The Creative Penn: the art of character](https://www.thecreativepenn.com/2013/04/15/art-of-character/amp/)

| Part | Question | Mercenary example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Want | What do they chase, on the surface? | Enough coin to buy back the family farm |
| Need | What do they really need, and avoid? | To stop running from the people they left behind |
| Wound | What happened that still hurts? | Their unit was sold out by a captain they trusted |
| Lie (misbelief) | What false belief came from the wound? | "Loyalty is a debt that someone always calls in" |
| Flaw | What behaviour grows from the lie? | Takes every contract alone and never shares a plan |
| Contradiction | Which trait goes against their main trait? | Cold to the company, but feeds every stray dog |
| Secret | What do they hide? | They still send coin to the captain's widow |

- **Link the parts.** The wound causes the lie. The lie causes the flaw. The flaw blocks the need. Characters feel flat when these parts are not linked. [No Film School: start with their biggest lie](https://nofilmschool.com/character-wound-and-misbelief)
- **Small traits give depth to big ones.** George Saunders calls this "cross-paint": a small trait painted across the main one. [UEA: how to create compelling characters](https://www.uea.ac.uk/creative/writing/how-to-write/how-to-create-compelling-characters)
- **Most backstory never appears.** Write it so that you know the character, but show only the wound and the flaw. Reveal the secret slowly, at the right moment.
- **An irrational behaviour with a clear emotional root** makes a character feel real. Without one, agents and language models write characters that are helpful and logical all the time.

## 3. Voice and behaviour

- Write the voice in rules: sentence length, words they use and never use, verbal tics, and what they do under stress. [Celtx: character bible](https://blog.celtx.com/how-to-create-a-character-bible/), [No Film School: character bio template](https://nofilmschool.com/character-bio-template)
- Write a "never" list: things this character never does or says. It keeps all later lines consistent.
- Write the barks by trigger: recruited, idle, attack, hit, low health, victory, evolution, dismissed. 2 lines for each trigger.
- Write the relationships: how they feel about the player's company, a rival and one faction, and why.

## 4. Shape and silhouette

- **The silhouette carries most of the design.** Fill the character in black. You must still know who it is and what it does. In Overwatch, the team asked whether a new silhouette was too close to an existing one. [PC Gamer: Torbjörn and Overwatch art](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-torbjrn-became-the-art-inspiration-for-all-of-overwatch), [Character readability in TF2 and Overwatch](https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465)
- **Shape language.** Circles read as friendly and soft. Squares read as stable and strong. Triangles read as fast, sharp or dangerous. Pick one main shape and one second shape for each character. [Rocketbrush: shape language](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent), [Creative Bloq: how shape makes character art](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/digital-art/how-shape-can-make-or-break-your-character-art)
- **The three reads.** First the silhouette, then the colour blocks, then the detail. The character must read at the far camera, the near camera and on the portrait.
- **The grayscale test.** The design must still read in grayscale. Colour supports the shape, but it does not replace it.
- **Exaggerate the traits that matter.** Overwatch pushed the main traits of each hero until the silhouettes became unique.
- **The role shows in the shape.** A tank, an archer and a healer look different, and they act differently too.

## 5. Costume, wear and props

- **Ask who made the gear, from what, and who repairs it.** A mercenary's armor is a mix: issued plates, looted pieces, field repairs. Each piece has a source. [Springhole: better costumes](https://springhole.net/art_tutorials/better-superhero-and-supervillain-costumes.htm)
- **Wear has a cause.** The bow arm wears the sleeve. The strap rubs the shoulder. The left side faces the enemy. The knees show where they kneel. Wear in random places looks fake. [Costume design breakdown](https://www.lafilm.edu/blog/a-breakdown-of-oscar-worthy-costume-design/)
- **Match the world.** Gear is believable when it fits the people around it. One character in impractical gear among armored soldiers looks false.
- **Design from function.** Start from the role in the game, the animations it needs and the camera distance. Larian cut flamboyant armor that the animators could not move in. [PC Gamer: Larian on Baldur's Gate 3 outfits](https://pcgamer.com/larians-art-director-on-the-outfits-of-baldurs-gate-3-and-why-making-them-fit-matters-but-the-main-thing-is-looking-cool)
- **Believable, not historical.** Players want gear that feels plausible, not a museum replica. Basic sense still applies: a joint must bend, and a quiver must be reachable.
- **Personal props carry the story.** One or two personal items: a token, a scar, a tattoo, a charm. Each one links to the wound or the secret. [Character costume design study guide](https://fiveable.me/art-direction/unit-11/character-costume-design/study-guide/sgNfVSZdvfNiXUfG)

## 6. Evolution stages

- **Each stage is a beat in the character's arc.** A costume change marks a turning point. The stages show the arc from the first look to the final, changed look. Write what happened to the character between two stages, not only that they are stronger.
- **Keep the identity anchors.** Keep the same face, the same main shape, the same main colours and one signature item at every stage. Blizzard kept Tracer's palette, silhouette and sense of motion in her redesign. [Overwatch 2: evolving the art](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/ru-ru/news/23189038)
- **Change in steps.** Add armor, size or sharper details from stage to stage. Keep the main shape of the body and the outfit. Change the details on it: softer edges suit an early stage, and sharper, more angular edges suit a late stage. [Creative Bloq: 30 years of Pokémon design](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/digital-art/what-artists-can-learn-from-30-years-of-pokemon-character-design)
- **Each stage needs a readable silhouette** that is different from the stage before, but still the same family. Similar outlines make characters hard to tell apart. [Smogon: Pokémon art over the years](https://www.smogon.com/articles/pokemon-art-through-ages)
- **Do not only add spikes and glow.** The last stage shows who the character became. It is not just a heavier version of the first.
- **Power is felt through new skills too.** In hero collectors, each tier unlocks something new. Pair each visual stage with one new skill or behaviour that the player can see. [AFK Journey ascension](https://www.destructoid.com/afk-journey-ascension-guide-and-costs/)

## 7. Motion and acting

- **The idle shows the personality.** An idle animation is proof of life when the player gives no input. The best idles show the character. Sonic taps his foot. [GamesRadar: the art of standing around](https://gamesradar.com/deceptively-complex-art-doing-nothing-standing-around), [Kotaku: the quiet importance of idle animations](https://kotaku.com/the-quiet-importance-of-idle-animations-1834564079)
- **Layer small fidgets** so the loop is hard to spot. A loop that repeats looks artificial. [Animschool: breathing life into idle animations](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/06/14/breathing-life-into-idle-animations)
- **The idle is the hub of all motion.** Every other clip starts from it and returns to it.
- Write 3 motion notes for each character: how they stand, how they move, and one habit. For example: "weight on the back foot, checks the bowstring when idle".
